Constitutional Bodies — EC, UPSC, CAG & Others: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 14 min read · BPSC SSC

Constitutional Bodies are institutions established directly by the Constitution of India. They derive their powers, functions and composition from specific Articles of the Constitution. They are independent of the executive and play a crucial role in the governance and democratic functioning of the country. The most important constitutional bodies are the Election Commission, UPSC, CAG, Finance Commission, Attorney General, Advocate General, and the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities. For BPSC and SSC, this topic is extremely high-yield — especially the composition and powers of the EC, UPSC and CAG.

Exam Tip: Constitutional Bodies are created by the Constitution itself — like the Election Commission (Article 324), UPSC (Article 315), CAG (Article 148). Non-Constitutional Bodies — like NITI Aayog, NHRC, CIC, CVC — are created by an Act of Parliament or executive order.

1. ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA (ECI)

Article 324 of the Constitution provides for the Election Commission.

Composition

  • Consists of a Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and two other Election Commissioners.
  • Originally, the EC had only one member — the CEC. Two additional commissioners were added in 1993.
  • The CEC and other ECs are appointed by the President.
  • The Chief Election Commissioner presides over the Commission.
  • The President appoints the CEC and other Election Commissioners.
  • Their tenure is 6 years or until they attain 65 years of age — whichever is earlier.

Removal of Election Commissioners

  • The CEC can be removed only by impeachment in the same manner as a Supreme Court judge.
  • Other Election Commissioners can be removed by the President on the recommendation of the CEC.
  • The CEC's removal requires a special majority in both Houses of Parliament.

Powers and Functions

  • Conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of the President and Vice-President.
  • Prepares and maintains the electoral rolls.
  • Recognises political parties and allots election symbols.
  • Enforces the Model Code of Conduct during elections.
  • Advisory jurisdiction in matters of disqualification of members.
  • Can postpone or countermand elections in specific circumstances.
  • Sets limits on election expenses.

Remember: The Election Commission consists of a Chief Election Commissioner and two other Election Commissioners. The CEC can only be removed by impeachment — like a Supreme Court judge. The first CEC was Sukumar Sen (1950). The Model Code of Conduct is a non-statutory but powerful tool used by the ECI.

2. UNION PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION (UPSC)

Article 315 provides for the Union Public Service Commission and State Public Service Commissions.

Composition

  • Consists of a Chairman and other members appointed by the President.
  • The number of members is determined by the President — usually 9 to 11 members.
  • Members hold office for a term of 6 years or until they attain the age of 65 years — whichever is earlier.
  • At least half of the members must be persons who have held office for at least 10 years under the Government of India or a State Government.

Removal of UPSC Members

  • The Chairman or a member can be removed only by the President on the grounds of:
    • Insolvency.
    • Paid employment outside the Commission.
    • Infirmity of mind or body.
    • Misbehaviour — after a reference to the Supreme Court.

Functions of UPSC

  • Conducts examinations for appointments to the All India Services and central services.
  • Assists in the selection of officers for Group A and Group B services.
  • Advises the government on matters of recruitment, promotion, transfer and disciplinary action.
  • Advises on reservation and other service conditions.
  • Presents an annual report to the President on its work.

State Public Service Commission (SPSC)

  • Every state has its own SPSC — established under Article 315.
  • Members hold office for 6 years or until they attain the age of 62 years — whichever is earlier.
  • Appointed by the Governor of the state.
  • Conducts exams for state government posts.

Bihar Public Service Commission (BPSC)

  • Bihar's SPSC — the Bihar Public Service Commission — conducts the BPSC Combined Competitive Examination (CCE).
  • Established in 1949 at Patna.
  • Its headquarters are in Patna, Bihar.
  • The BPSC conducts examinations for recruitment to various state government posts.
BPSC Must-Know: The UPSC conducts exams for All India Services and central services — while the BPSC (Bihar Public Service Commission) conducts exams for state government posts. UPSC members retire at 65 years; SPSC members retire at 62 years. This is a very high-yield fact.

3. COMPTROLLER AND AUDITOR GENERAL (CAG)

Articles 148 to 151 provide for the Comptroller and Auditor General of India — the guardian of the public purse.

Appointment and Tenure

  • Appointed by the President.
  • Holds office for 6 years or until attaining the age of 65 years — whichever is earlier.
  • Can resign by writing to the President.
  • Can be removed only by impeachment — in the same manner as a Supreme Court judge.
  • His salary and conditions of service are determined by Parliament — but cannot be varied to his disadvantage during his tenure.
  • After retirement, he is not eligible for any further office under the Government of India or any State Government.

Functions of CAG

  • Audits all receipts and expenditure of the Government of India and the States.
  • Audits the accounts of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) and government companies.
  • Audits the accounts of autonomous bodies substantially financed by the government.
  • Reports to the President (for Union accounts) and Governor (for State accounts).
  • Reports are laid before Parliament and the State Legislature.
  • Advises the government on accounting matters.
  • Compiles and maintains the accounts of State Governments (though many states have separated this function).

Narahari Rao. His removal requires the same procedure as a Supreme Court judge — this protects his independence.

4. FINANCE COMMISSION

Article 280 provides for the Finance Commission of India.

Composition

  • Consists of a Chairman and four other members.
  • The Chairman and members are appointed by the President.
  • The Chairman should be a person who has experience in public affairs.
  • Members are experts in finance, administration, economics, and public affairs.

Functions

  • Recommends the distribution of net proceeds of taxes between the Centre and the States.
  • Recommends the principles governing grants-in-aid to States from the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • Recommends measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State for panchayats and municipalities.
  • Any other matter referred to it by the President.

Important Facts

  • Constituted every 5 years.
  • The first Finance Commission was constituted in 1951 — chaired by K.C. Neogy.
  • The 15th Finance Commission was chaired by N.K. Singh (2020–2025).
  • The 16th Finance Commission has been constituted under the chairmanship of Arvind Panagariya (2024).
  • Recommendations are advisory — not binding.

5. ATTORNEY GENERAL OF INDIA

Article 76 provides for the Attorney General of India.

Appointment and Tenure

  • Appointed by the President.
  • Must be qualified to be a judge of the Supreme Court (i.e., a citizen of India, and either a judge of a High Court for 5 years, an advocate of a High Court for 10 years, or a distinguished jurist).
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President — no fixed term.
  • Can resign by writing to the President.

Functions

  • The first law officer of the Government of India.
  • Advises the Government of India on legal matters.
  • Appears for the Government of India in the Supreme Court in important cases.
  • Has the right of audience in all courts in India.
  • Has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of both Houses of Parliament — but without the right to vote.
  • Can be a member of any committee of Parliament — but has no right to vote.

Solicitor General of India

  • The second law officer of the Government of India.
  • Assists the Attorney General.
  • Appointed by the Government of India — not a constitutional post.

6. ADVOCATE GENERAL OF THE STATE

Article 165 provides for the Advocate General of the State.

Appointment and Tenure

  • Appointed by the Governor of the state.
  • Must be qualified to be a judge of a High Court.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor — no fixed term.

Functions

  • The first law officer of the State Government.
  • Advises the State Government on legal matters.
  • Appears for the State Government in the High Court and the Supreme Court.
  • Has the right of audience in all courts in the state.
  • Has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of the State Legislature — but without the right to vote.

7. SPECIAL OFFICER FOR LINGUISTIC MINORITIES

Article 350B provides for a Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities — appointed by the President.

Functions

  • Investigates all matters relating to the safeguards provided for linguistic minorities.
  • Reports to the President on these matters.
  • Reports are laid before Parliament.
  • The Commissioner is assisted by Deputy Commissioners and Assistant Commissioners in different states.

8. NATIONAL COMMISSIONS FOR SCs, STs AND OBCs

National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)

  • Established under Article 338.
  • Consists of a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and three other members.
  • Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 3 years.
  • Investigates and monitors safeguards for SCs.
  • Advises on socio-economic development of SCs.

National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)

  • Established under Article 338A — inserted by the 89th Amendment Act, 2003.
  • Consists of a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and three other members.
  • Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 3 years.
  • Monitors safeguards for STs and advises on their development.

National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)

  • Given constitutional status by the 102nd Amendment Act, 2018 — added Article 338B.
  • Consists of a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and three other members.
  • Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 3 years.
  • Investigates and monitors safeguards for socially and educationally backward classes (SEBCs).
Remember: NCSC — Article 338 — for Scheduled Castes.
NCST — Article 338A (89th Amendment, 2003) — for Scheduled Tribes.
NCBC — Article 338B (102nd Amendment, 2018) — for Backward Classes.

9. GST COUNCIL

Article 279A — inserted by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016.

Composition

  • Chairperson — Union Finance Minister.
  • Members — Union Minister of State for Finance, and one Minister from each State — nominated by the State Government.

Functions

  • Makes recommendations on GST rates, exemptions, and threshold limits.
  • Recommends special provisions for special category states.
  • Recommends the date from which GST will be levied on petroleum products.
  • Decisions require a 3/4 majority — with the Centre having 1/3 weight and States having 2/3 weight.

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

Bihar Public Service Commission (BPSC)

  • Bihar Public Service Commission — established in 1949.
  • Headquarters: Patna, Bihar.
  • Conducts the BPSC Combined Competitive Examination for recruitment to Bihar state services.
  • The BPSC is one of the most respected state public service commissions in India — with lakhs of aspirants appearing every year.
  • The BPSC CCE (Prelims and Mains) is the flagship exam for entry into Bihar's administrative services.
  • The BPSC Chairman and members are appointed by the Governor of Bihar.
  • BPSC members retire at the age of 62 years.

Bihar State Election Commission

  • Constituted under Article 243K.
  • Conducts Panchayat and Municipal elections in Bihar.
  • Bihar's State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor.
  • Bihar conducted Panchayat elections in 2021 — with over 1 lakh candidates contesting across the state.

Bihar State Finance Commission

  • Constituted under Article 243I.
  • Reviews the financial position of Panchayats and Municipalities in Bihar.
  • Recommends distribution of taxes between the state and local bodies.
  • Bihar has constituted several State Finance Commissions — each serving for 5 years.

Bihar — Advocate General

  • The Advocate General of Bihar is the first law officer of the state.
  • Appointed by the Governor of Bihar.
  • Represents the Bihar Government in the Patna High Court and the Supreme Court.

Bihar — Key Constitutional Facts

  • Bihar has 40 Lok Sabha seats and 16 Rajya Sabha seats.
  • Bihar Vidhan Sabha — 243 seats; Vidhan Parishad — 75 seats.
  • Patna High Court — established 1 March 1916 — one of the oldest High Courts in India.
  • Bihar was the first state to implement 50% reservation for women in Panchayati Raj institutions (2006).
  • Bihar Diwas — celebrated on 22 March — the day Bihar became a separate province in 1912.
  • First Chief Minister of Bihar — Shri Krishna Sinha (1937–1961).
  • First President of India from Bihar — Dr. Rajendra Prasad (Zeradei, Siwan).
BPSC Must-Know: The Bihar Public Service Commission (BPSC) was established in 1949 with headquarters at Patna. The Bihar State Election Commission conducts Panchayat elections. The Bihar State Finance Commission reviews the finances of local bodies. The Advocate General of Bihar represents the state in the Patna High Court and Supreme Court.
