Preamble & Basic Structure Doctrine: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 11 min read · BPSC SSC

The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Indian Constitution. It declares the source of authority (the people), the nature of the State (sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic, republic), and the objectives (justice, liberty, equality, fraternity). The Basic Structure Doctrine — established by the Supreme Court in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) — holds that Parliament cannot amend the "basic structure" of the Constitution. For BPSC and SSC, both topics are extremely high-yield.

The Preamble — Text

The Preamble reads:

"WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;
and to promote among them all
FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION."

Key Words in the Preamble

TermMeaning
SovereignIndia is free to conduct its own affairs — internal and external. No external authority controls it.
SocialistWealth should be distributed fairly; the State should reduce inequality. Added by 42nd Amendment (1976).
SecularIndia has no official religion. The State treats all religions equally. Added by 42nd Amendment (1976).
DemocraticGovernment is elected by the people; universal adult suffrage; free and fair elections.
RepublicThe head of state (President) is elected, not hereditary.
JusticeSocial, economic and political — borrowed from the Soviet Constitution (USSR).
LibertyOf thought, expression, belief, faith, worship — from the French Revolution.
EqualityOf status and opportunity — from the French Revolution.
FraternityBrotherhood; assures dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the nation — from the French Revolution.
Exam Tip: The words "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" were added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 — during the Emergency. This is one of the most frequently asked questions in BPSC and SSC exams.

Is the Preamble Part of the Constitution?

This question was debated for decades. The Supreme Court's position evolved through several judgments:

CaseYearRuling
Berubari Union case1960Preamble is not a part of the Constitution
Kesavananda Bharati case1973Preamble is a part of the Constitution; can be amended
LIC of India case1995Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution

Final Position

  • The Preamble is a part of the Constitution (as per the Kesavananda Bharati and LIC judgments).
  • It can be amended under Article 368 — but only if the amendment does not violate the basic structure.
  • It is not enforceable in a court of law — the Preamble itself does not grant any rights.
  • However, it can be used as an aid to interpretation when the meaning of a provision is unclear.
Remember: The Preamble has been amended only once — by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, which added the words "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity". No other amendment has touched the Preamble.

Objectives of the Preamble

  • Source of authority: "We, the People of India" — the ultimate authority is the people.
  • Nature of the State: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
  • Objectives: Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.
  • Date of adoption: 26 November 1949.

Basic Structure Doctrine — Background

The Basic Structure Doctrine is one of the most important principles of Indian constitutional law. It holds that Parliament cannot amend the Constitution in a way that destroys its essential features — even though Parliament has wide amending powers under Article 368.

Evolution of the Doctrine

Sajjan Singh case1965Parliament has wide amending powers; 17th Amendment upheld
Golaknath case1967Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights

Minerva Mills case1980Reaffirmed Basic Structure; struck down parts of 42nd Amendment
Waman Rao case1981Basic Structure applies to amendments after 24 April 1973

Exam Tip: The Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) is the most important constitutional case in Indian history. A 13-judge bench of the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure. The case arose from a challenge to the Kerala Land Reforms Act.

What is the "Basic Structure"?

The Supreme Court has not defined the Basic Structure exhaustively — it has instead listed elements over time. Some of the key elements identified in various judgments are:

  • Supremacy of the Constitution
  • Republican and Democratic form of government
  • Secular character of the Constitution
  • Separation of powers between Legislature, Executive and Judiciary
  • Federal character of the Constitution
  • Unity and integrity of the nation
  • Judicial review — the power of courts to review laws
  • Rule of law
  • Free and fair elections
  • Independence of the judiciary
  • Fundamental Rights — especially Articles 14, 19 and 21
  • Sovereignty of India
  • Dignity of the individual
  • Welfare state
  • Equality — social, economic and political
  • Secularism
  • Effective access to justice
  • Limited amending power of Parliament

Article 368 — Amending Power of Parliament

Article 368 of the Constitution deals with the procedure for amendment. It states that Parliament may amend the Constitution by a special majority — but the Supreme Court has held that this power is subject to the Basic Structure Doctrine.

Types of Amendments

TypeProcedureExample
Simple MajorityLike passing an ordinary lawCreation of new states, citizenship, salaries
Special Majority2/3 of members present and voting + majority of total membership of each HouseFundamental Rights, DPSP, Article 368 itself
Special Majority + Ratification by StatesSpecial majority + ratification by half the statesElection of President, distribution of legislative powers, Supreme Court/High Court
BPSC Must-Know: The Basic Structure Doctrine limits Parliament's amending power under Article 368. It was established in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973). Any amendment that destroys the basic structure is void — even if passed by a special majority.

Important Judgments on Basic Structure

Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

  • Petitioned by Sri Kesavananda Bharati, head of the Edneer Mutt in Kerala.
  • Challenged the Kerala Land Reforms Act — specifically the 24th and 25th Amendments.
  • A 13-judge bench (the largest ever) delivered the judgment by a 7:6 majority.
  • Held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure.
  • The case established the Basic Structure Doctrine — a landmark in constitutional law.

Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)

  • The Supreme Court applied the Basic Structure Doctrine to strike down part of the 39th Amendment.
  • The 39th Amendment had placed the election of the Prime Minister and Speaker beyond judicial review.
  • The Court held that free and fair elections are part of the Basic Structure.

Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)

  • Struck down parts of the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976.
  • Held that the harmony between Fundamental Rights and DPSP is part of the Basic Structure.
  • Also struck down the provision that gave Parliament unlimited amending power.

Waman Rao v. Union of India (1981)

  • Held that the Basic Structure Doctrine applies to all amendments made after 24 April 1973 — the date of the Kesavananda judgment.

I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007)

  • Held that laws placed in the Ninth Schedule can be challenged if they violate the Basic Structure.
  • The Ninth Schedule was originally meant to protect land reform laws from judicial review — but the Court ruled that this protection is not absolute.

Significance of the Basic Structure Doctrine

  • Protects the Constitution from being destroyed by a temporary majority in Parliament.
  • Preserves the rights of the people — especially Fundamental Rights.
  • Maintains the balance of power between Parliament and the Judiciary.
  • Ensures the supremacy of the Constitution — not of Parliament.
  • Protects the federal character of India.
  • Upholds the rule of law and constitutionalism.

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

  • The Preamble was adopted at the Constituent Assembly — whose permanent President was Dr. Rajendra Prasad from Bihar.
  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha (interim Chairman) was from Bihar — he presided over the very first session of the Constituent Assembly.
  • Bihar was one of the provinces represented in the Constituent Assembly — and its members participated in the debates on the Preamble and Basic Structure.
  • The Basic Structure Doctrine has been invoked in several cases relating to Bihar — including cases involving reservation policies and land reforms.
  • The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 — which added "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" to the Preamble — was passed during the Emergency. This was a period when civil liberties were suspended across India, including in Bihar.
BPSC Must-Know: Dr. Rajendra Prasad (Bihar) presided over the Constituent Assembly that adopted the Preamble. The 42nd Amendment (1976) — which added "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" to the Preamble — was a landmark moment in Indian constitutional history.
