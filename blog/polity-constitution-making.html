Making of the Indian Constitution: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 12 min read · BPSC SSC

The Indian Constitution is the longest written constitution in the world. It was framed by the Constituent Assembly over a period of 2 years, 11 months and 18 days — from 9 December 1946 to 26 November 1949. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, as the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, is called the "Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution." For BPSC and SSC, this topic is essential — the Constituent Assembly, its committees, and the sources of the Constitution are among the most frequently asked questions.

Historical Background

The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first proposed by M.N. Roy in 1934. The demand was later taken up by the Indian National Congress and became a major political issue during the freedom struggle.

YearEvent
1934M.N. Roy first proposed the idea of a Constituent Assembly
1935Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly
1938Jawaharlal Nehru declared that the Constitution of India would be framed by a Constituent Assembly elected on adult franchise
1940August Offer — British government accepted the demand in principle

1946Cabinet Mission Plan — finalised the scheme for the Constituent Assembly
Exam Tip: The idea of a Constituent Assembly was first proposed by M.N. Roy in 1934. The demand was officially accepted by the British in the August Offer (1940) and the scheme was finalised in the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946).

Composition of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly was constituted in November 1946 under the scheme formulated by the Cabinet Mission Plan. It was a partly elected and partly nominated body.

  • Total strength: 389 members (initially).
  • Provincial Legislatures: 296 seats (elected by members of provincial legislative assemblies).
  • Princely States: 93 seats (nominated by the rulers of princely states).
  • Representation was based on population — roughly one member per million people.
  • Communal representation — seats were allotted to Muslims, Sikhs and other minorities.
  • After Partition (1947), the strength was reduced to 299 members — 229 from provinces, 70 from princely states.

Elections to the Constituent Assembly

  • Elections were held in July–August 1946.
  • The Indian National Congress won 208 seats; the Muslim League won 73 seats.
  • The Muslim League initially refused to participate — it demanded a separate Pakistan.
  • After Partition, the Muslim League members withdrew from the Assembly.

Key Objectives

  • To frame a Constitution for independent India.
  • To establish a sovereign, democratic republic.
  • To guarantee justice, liberty, equality and fraternity to all citizens.

Important Sessions of the Constituent Assembly

SessionDateKey Events
First Session9 December 1946Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha elected as interim Chairman; Muslim League absent
Second Session11 December 1946Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected as permanent President; Objectives Resolution moved by Nehru
Third Session13 December 1946Objectives Resolution adopted (22 January 1947)

Remember: Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was the interim Chairman of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the permanent President. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.

Objectives Resolution

The Objectives Resolution was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946. It laid down the fundamental principles and philosophy of the Constitution. It was adopted on 22 January 1947.

Key Features of the Objectives Resolution

  • India was to be an Independent Sovereign Republic.
  • All power and authority were to be derived from the people.
  • Guarantees of justice, social, economic and political.
  • Guarantees of equality of status, opportunity and before law.
  • Guarantees of freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation, association and action.
  • Adequate safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas, and depressed classes.
  • Maintenance of the integrity of the territory and the sovereign rights of the Republic on land, sea and air.
  • Promotion of world peace and the welfare of mankind.
Exam Tip: The Objectives Resolution was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru and adopted on 22 January 1947. It is considered the philosophical foundation of the Indian Constitution — its spirit is reflected in the Preamble.

BPSC Must-Know: The Drafting Committee was the most important committee, chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar. It had 7 members: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman), Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter), and T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan).

The Drafting Committee

The Drafting Committee was set up on 29 August 1947 to prepare a draft of the Constitution. It was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

Seven Members of the Drafting Committee

  1. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar — Chairman
  2. Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
  3. N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar
  4. K.M. Munshi
  5. Syed Mohammad Saadulla
  6. N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter)
  7. T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan)

The Drafting Committee submitted the draft Constitution to the President of the Constituent Assembly on 21 February 1948.

Adoption of the Constitution

  • The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949.
  • The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950 — celebrated as Republic Day.
  • 26 January was chosen because on this day in 1930, the Indian National Congress had declared Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence).
  • On 26 January 1950, the Constituent Assembly became the Provisional Parliament of India.
  • The last session of the Constituent Assembly was held on 24 January 1950 — Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the first President of India.
Exam Tip: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. 26 November is celebrated as Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) in India — declared in 2015.

Sources of the Indian Constitution

The Indian Constitution borrowed features from many countries. Here are the major sources:

SourceFeatures Borrowed
Government of India Act, 1935Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, public service commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details
British ConstitutionParliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship, cabinet system, writs, bicameralism
US ConstitutionFundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of judiciary, impeachment of President, removal of judges
Irish ConstitutionDirective Principles of State Policy, nomination of members to Rajya Sabha, method of election of President
Canadian ConstitutionFederation with a strong Centre, appointment of Governors by the Centre, advisory jurisdiction of Supreme Court
Australian ConstitutionConcurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, joint sitting of the two Houses
Weimar Constitution of GermanySuspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency
Soviet Constitution (USSR)Fundamental Duties, ideal of justice (social, economic, political) in the Preamble
French ConstitutionRepublic, ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity in the Preamble
South African ConstitutionProcedure for amendment of the Constitution, election of Rajya Sabha members
Japanese ConstitutionProcedure established by law
BPSC Must-Know: The Government of India Act, 1935 is the single largest source of the Indian Constitution. The Directive Principles were borrowed from Ireland. Fundamental Duties came from the USSR. Fundamental Rights came from the USA. The Parliamentary system came from Britain.

Criticism of the Constituent Assembly

  • It was not a sovereign body — it was created by the British government.
  • It was not elected on the basis of adult franchise — only 28% of the adult population could vote.
  • It was dominated by the Congress — one-party domination.
  • It was dominated by lawyers and politicians — lacked representation from all sections.
  • It took a long time (2 years, 11 months, 18 days) — the longest in the world for a constitution to be framed.

Significance of the Constituent Assembly

  • It was a representative body — included members from all major communities, regions, and ideologies.
  • It produced the longest written constitution in the world — with 395 Articles and 8 Schedules (at the time of adoption).
  • It established the framework of parliamentary democracy in India.
  • It provided for Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties.
  • It created the foundation for a federal system with a strong Centre.
  • It ensured universal adult suffrage from the very first election (1951–52).
  • It provided for reservation for SCs, STs and OBCs — social justice.

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha — the interim Chairman of the Constituent Assembly — was from Bihar (born in Arrah, Bihar). He was the first Indian to preside over the Constituent Assembly.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad — the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly — was from Bihar (born in Zeradei, Siwan district). He became the first President of India.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar — Chairman of the Drafting Committee — though not from Bihar, was elected from Bihar to the Constituent Assembly. He later represented Bombay in the Constituent Assembly.
  • Several other members from Bihar contributed to the Constituent Assembly — including Syed Mohammad Saadulla (a member of the Drafting Committee) and Brajeswar Prasad.
  • Sachchidananda Sinha — the first session of the Constituent Assembly was held in the Constitutional Hall (now Parliament House) under his chairmanship.
  • The Constitution of India was adopted on 26 November 1949 — the Constituent Assembly sat for 165 days in 11 sessions over 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.
BPSC Must-Know: Two of the most important figures of the Constituent Assembly were from Bihar — Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha (interim Chairman, from Arrah) and Dr. Rajendra Prasad (permanent President, from Siwan). This is a key BPSC-favourite topic.

Fascinating Facts about the Constitution

  • Time taken: 2 years, 11 months, 18 days.
  • Total sittings: 165 days.
  • Number of sessions: 11.
  • Original Articles: 395.
  • Original Schedules: 8.
  • Original Parts: 22.
  • Cost of preparation: ₹64 lakh (a small amount even then!).
  • Currency of first meeting: 9 December 1946.
  • Last session: 24 January 1950.
  • Handwritten or printed: Both — the original was handwritten in Hindi and English; calligraphed by Prem Behari Narain Raizada.
  • Signatories: 284 members signed the final draft.
  • Longest constitution: The Indian Constitution is the longest written constitution in the world.
Exam Tip: The original Constitution was handwritten in Hindi and English and calligraphed by Prem Behari Narain Raizada. It is now preserved in a special helium-filled case in the Parliament Library.
