Chola Vansh: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 13 min read · BPSC SSC

The Chola Dynasty was one of the longest-ruling dynasties in Indian history and the greatest of the Sangam-age Tamil kingdoms. They ruled over Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and even parts of Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. Under Rajaraja Chola I and Rajendra Chola I, the Cholas built a powerful maritime empire and left behind magnificent temples like the Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur. For BPSC and SSC, the Cholas are the most important dynasty of South India.

Exam Tip: The Uttaramerur Inscription of Parantaka I is the single most important source for Chola local self-government — the Kudavolai (pot-ticket) system of election. This is a very common exam question.

Origin & Early Cholas

  • The Cholas are mentioned in Sangam literature and in Ashokan inscriptions (as "Choda").
  • Early Chola capital: Uraiyur (near Trichy, Tamil Nadu).
  • The greatest early Chola king was Karikala Chola, who built the Grand Anicut (Kallanai) — one of the oldest water-diversion structures still in use.
  • Early Cholas declined by the 4th century CE, overshadowed by the Pallavas and Pandyas.

Medieval Cholas — Revival

The Chola power was revived by Vijayalaya Chola in c. 850 CE, who captured Thanjavur from the Muttaraiyars and made it his capital. This began the Imperial Chola period.

Important Rulers Before Rajaraja I

  • Aditya I (c. 871–907 CE) — defeated the Pallavas and extended Chola territory.
  • Parantaka I (c. 907–955 CE) — the Uttaramerur Inscription belongs to his reign; took the title Maduraikonda (conqueror of Madurai).
  • Rajaraja I's predecessors — Uttama Chola ruled until 985 CE.

Rajaraja Chola I (985–1014 CE)

The true founder of the Imperial Chola Empire — a brilliant administrator, warrior and temple-builder.

  • Titles: Rajaraja, Arulmozhivarman, Rajakesari, Mummudi Cholan.
  • Conquered the Pandyas, Cheras, and parts of Sri Lanka (Ceylon).
  • Annexed northern Sri Lanka and named it Mummudichola Mandalam.
  • Captured the Maldives (then called Mandam).
  • Defeated the Western Chalukyas at the Battle of Donur.
  • Built the magnificent Brihadeshwara Temple (also called Rajarajeswara Temple) at Thanjavur — a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • Introduced the Valanadu — a new administrative unit between the district and the village.
  • Ordered a comprehensive land survey and standardised revenue assessment.
  • Patronised Saivism; also tolerant of Vaishnavism and Buddhism.
Remember: The Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur was built by Rajaraja Chola I and is dedicated to Lord Shiva. It is also called Peruvudaiyar Kovil and Rajarajeswaram. Its Vimana (tower) is about 66 metres tall — one of the tallest in India.

Rajendra Chola I (1014–1044 CE)

Son of Rajaraja I; the greatest conqueror of the Chola dynasty and the only Indian king to lead a naval expedition across the sea.

  • Titles: Rajendra, Gangaikondan, Kadaram Kondan, Mudikondan, Pandita Cholan.
  • Led a famous naval expedition to Southeast Asia in 1025 CE — attacked Srivijaya (modern Indonesia/Malaysia) and Kadaram (Kedah).
  • Defeated Mahipala, the Pala king of Bengal, and took the title Gangaikondan (conqueror of the Ganga).
  • Built a new capital at Gangaikondacholapuram ("City of the Chola who conquered the Ganga") to commemorate his victory.
  • Built the Gangaikondacholapuram Temple — a masterpiece of Chola architecture.
  • Completed the conquest of Sri Lanka.
  • His Thiruvalangadu and Karanthai inscriptions record his conquests in detail.
Exam Tip: Rajendra Chola I's naval expedition to Kadaram (1025 CE) is a landmark event. He was the only Indian ruler to launch a maritime campaign across the Bay of Bengal. His titles Kadaram Kondan and Gangaikondan are frequently asked.

Later Cholas

  • Rajadhiraja Chola I (1018–1054 CE) — son of Rajendra I; died at the Battle of Koppam against the Western Chalukyas.
  • Rajendra Chola II (1054–1063 CE) — continued the struggle with the Chalukyas.
  • Virarajendra Chola (1063–1070 CE) — successful against the Chalukyas.
  • Athirajendra Chola (1070 CE) — last of the direct line; his death led to a succession crisis.
  • Kulottunga Chola I (1070–1122 CE) — the greatest of the later Cholas; also known as Rajendra Chola. He was the son of Rajaraja Narendra (Eastern Chalukya) and Ammangai (Chola princess). His reign saw peace, prosperity and administrative reforms.
  • After Kulottunga I, the Chola power steadily declined.

Chola Administration

Central Administration

  • The king was the head of state — assisted by a council of ministers.
  • Officials: Perundaram (higher officials), Sirutaram (lower officials).
  • The empire was divided into Mandalam (province) → Valanadu → Nadu → Kurram → Village.
  • Rajendra I introduced the Valanadu as an intermediary unit.

Local Self-Government (Chola Speciality)

The Cholas are famous for their highly developed system of local self-government, especially the village assemblies.

  • Ur — general village assembly (for all castes).
  • Sabha (or Mahasabha) — assembly of the Brahmin villages (Agraharas).
  • Nagaram — assembly of merchant towns.
  • Kudavolai system — election by drawing lots (pot-tickets); described in the Uttaramerur Inscription.
  • Committees of the Sabha: Variyam — including the famous Annual Committee, Garden Committee, Tank Committee.
BPSC Must-Know: The Uttaramerur Inscription describes the Kudavolai system of election to the village Sabha — the world's earliest documented example of democratic local self-government. This is a favourite question in both BPSC and SSC exams.

Revenue Administration

  • Land revenue was the main source of income — usually 1/3 to 1/6 of produce.
  • Land was classified as Vellanvagai (ryot land), Brahmadeya (gift to Brahmins), Shalabhoga (land for schools), Devadana (gift to temples), Pallichchandam (gift to Jain institutions).
  • Comprehensive land survey conducted under Rajaraja I.
  • Taxes on trade, crafts, irrigation, fishing and grazing.

Key Features of Chola Architecture

  • Vimana — the tall, pyramidal tower over the sanctum, made of stone.
  • Gopuram — the entrance gateway; became more prominent in later Chola and Vijayanagara periods.
  • Mandapa — pillared hall in front of the sanctum.
  • Beautiful bronze sculptures — especially the Nataraja (dancing Shiva) and Parvati.
  • Chola bronzes are considered the finest in the world — made using the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique.
Exam Tip: The Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (part of the "Great Living Chola Temples"). The Nataraja bronze from the Chola period is one of India's most iconic art pieces.

Literature & Culture

  • Kamban — wrote the Kamba Ramayanam (Tamil Ramayana) during Kulottunga Chola III's reign.
  • Ottakoothar — court poet of Kulottunga II and Rajaraja II; wrote Kulottunga Cholan Ula.
  • Pugalendi — another famous poet.
  • Sekkilar — wrote Periyapuranam (hagiography of Shaiva saints) in the Chola period.
  • Tamil literature flourished — Cholas patronised Tamil, unlike the Pallavas who patronised Sanskrit.
  • Religion: Primarily Shaiva, but they were tolerant of Vaishnavism, Buddhism, and Jainism.

Trade & Maritime Power

  • Cholas controlled the Malabar and Coromandel coasts — major trade routes.
  • Trade with Southeast Asia, China, Arab countries — spices, textiles, ivory, pearls.
  • Trade guilds: Manigramam and Nanadesi.
  • Nagapattinam was the chief port — a flourishing centre of international trade.
  • Rajendra I's naval expedition to Srivijaya protected Chola trade interests in the Malacca Strait.

Decline of the Chola Empire

  • Weak successors after Kulottunga I — no able ruler could hold the empire together.
  • Rise of the Pandyas in the south — under Jatavarman Sundara Pandya, they defeated the Cholas in the late 13th century.
  • Hoysalas in the west and Kakatiyas in the east grew stronger.
  • Constant wars with the Western Chalukyas drained resources.
  • Loss of Sri Lanka — the Sinhalese regained control under Vijayabahu I.
  • Delhi Sultanate invasions under Malik Kafur (Alauddin Khilji's general) in 1310–11 CE delivered the final blow.
  • The last Chola ruler, Rajendra Chola III, ruled until c. 1279 CE, after which the dynasty disappeared from history.
