Agriculture & Food Security: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 14 min read · BPSC SSC

Agriculture is the backbone of the Indian economy. It employs nearly 45% of the workforce and contributes about 18% of GDP. Food security — ensuring that every citizen has access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food — is a fundamental concern. The Green Revolution, MSP, PDS, and the National Food Security Act are all critical to this. For BPSC and SSC, this topic is extremely high-yield — especially for Bihar, which is an agriculture-dominated state.

Importance of Agriculture in India

  • Employment: ~45% of the workforce depends on agriculture.
  • GDP Share: ~18% of India's GDP.
  • Food Security: Provides food for over 1.4 billion people.
  • Raw Materials: Supplies raw materials to industries (cotton, jute, sugarcane).
  • Export Earnings: Agricultural products contribute significantly to exports.
  • Rural Livelihood: Supports rural economy and cottage industries.
  • Industrial Linkage: Provides market for industrial goods (fertilisers, tractors).

Exam Tip: Kharif crops are sown with the monsoon (June–July) — rice is the main Kharif crop. Rabi crops are sown in winter (Oct–Dec) — wheat is the main Rabi crop. Zaid is the summer season — for fruits and vegetables.

Green Revolution

The Green Revolution was a period of rapid agricultural growth in India — from the mid-1960s to the 1980s — driven by high-yielding varieties (HYV) of seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and irrigation.

Key Features

  • Started in: 1966–67 — under the leadership of Dr. M.S. Swaminathan.
  • Main Crops: Wheat and rice — the focus was on food grains.
  • Main States: Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh.
  • Technologies: HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, mechanisation, irrigation.
  • Impact: India became self-sufficient in food grains — ended the "ship-to-mouth" era.

Father of Green Revolution

  • Global: Norman Borlaug — Nobel Peace Prize (1970).
  • India: Dr. M.S. Swaminathan — the chief architect of India's Green Revolution.
  • Dr. Verghese Kurien — Father of White Revolution (Operation Flood).

Criticism of Green Revolution

  • Regional Disparity: Benefited mainly Punjab, Haryana, Western UP — not Eastern India.
  • Ecological Damage: Soil degradation, water depletion, loss of biodiversity.
  • Inequality: Benefited large farmers more than small farmers.
  • Crop Imbalance: Focused on wheat and rice — neglected pulses and oilseeds.
  • Health Concerns: Excessive use of pesticides and fertilisers.

Minimum Support Price (MSP)

  • MSP is the minimum price at which the government promises to purchase crops from farmers — to protect them from market fluctuations.
  • Recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP).
  • Announced by the Ministry of Agriculture.
  • Based on the A2+FL formula — cost of production plus imputed family labour.
  • The Swaminathan Commission (2006) recommended MSP at 50% above the C2 cost (comprehensive cost).
  • Currently, MSP is announced for 23 crops — 7 cereals, 5 pulses, 7 oilseeds, 4 commercial crops.
  • Procurement — mainly for wheat and rice — done by FCI and state agencies.

BPSC Must-Know: MSP is recommended by the CACP and announced by the Ministry of Agriculture. It is based on the A2+FL formula. The Swaminathan Commission (2006) recommended MSP at 50% above the C2 cost — this recommendation has not been fully implemented. MSP is currently announced for 23 crops.

Food Security in India

Food security means that all people, at all times, have physical, social and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food.

Three Pillars of Food Security

  • Availability — sufficient food production.
  • Accessibility — physical and economic access.
  • Affordability — ability to purchase food.

Public Distribution System (PDS)

  • The PDS is the backbone of India's food security system.
  • Operated by the Food Corporation of India (FCI) and state governments.
  • Provides subsidised food grains to the poor through Fair Price Shops.
  • Evolved through several phases:
    • Revamped PDS (1992) — focused on hilly, remote areas.
    • Targeted PDS (1997) — two-tier system — BPL and APL.
    • Antyodaya Anna Yojana (2000) — for the poorest of the poor.
    • National Food Security Act (2013) — legal right to food.

National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013

  • Also known as the Right to Food Act.
  • Provides legal entitlement to subsidised food grains.
  • Covers up to 75% of the rural population and 50% of the urban population.
  • Entitlements:
    • Priority Households (PHH) — 5 kg per person per month.
    • Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) — 35 kg per household per month.
  • Prices: Rice at ₹3/kg, Wheat at ₹2/kg, Coarse grains at ₹1/kg.
  • Provides for women as head of household for issuing ration cards.
  • Includes provisions for maternity benefits and child nutrition.
Exam Tip: The NFSA, 2013 covers up to 75% of the rural population and 50% of the urban population. Under it, Priority Households get 5 kg per person per month, while AAY households get 35 kg per household per month. Prices: Rice ₹3/kg, Wheat ₹2/kg, Coarse grains ₹1/kg.

Food Corporation of India (FCI)

  • Set up in 1965 under the Food Corporations Act, 1964.
  • Objectives:
    • Effective price support operations for food grains.
    • Distribution of food grains throughout the country.
    • Maintenance of buffer stocks.
  • Works under the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution.
  • Procurement mainly for wheat and rice at MSP.

Important Government Schemes for Agriculture

Income Support

  • PM-KISAN (2019) — ₹6,000 per year to small and marginal farmers in three instalments.
  • PM Fasal Bima Yojana (2016) — crop insurance.
  • Kisan Credit Card (KCC) — credit for farmers.

Irrigation & Infrastructure

  • Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY) — "Har Khet Ko Pani".
  • Micro Irrigation Fund — for drip and sprinkler irrigation.
  • Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) — for agricultural development.

Marketing & Procurement

  • e-NAM (National Agriculture Market) — online trading platform for agricultural commodities.
  • MSP Procurement — government procurement at MSP.
  • APMC Reforms — state-level agricultural marketing reforms.

Soil & Nutrition

  • Soil Health Card Scheme (2015) — for balanced use of fertilisers.
  • Neem Coated Urea — to reduce urea consumption.
  • Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana — organic farming.

Food Security

  • National Food Security Act (2013).
  • Antyodaya Anna Yojana (2000).
  • Mid-Day Meal Scheme — nutrition for school children.
  • Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) — for children and mothers.
  • PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (2020) — free food grains during COVID-19.
BPSC Must-Know: PM-KISAN provides ₹6,000 per year to small and marginal farmers. PM Fasal Bima Yojana is crop insurance. e-NAM is the online agricultural marketing platform. Soil Health Card promotes balanced fertiliser use. PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (2020) provided free food grains during COVID-19.

Agricultural Marketing & Reforms

  • APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) — regulated markets for agricultural produce.
  • e-NAM — online platform connecting APMC mandis across India.
  • Model APMC Act — suggested reforms to allow direct buying from farmers.
  • Contract Farming — agreement between farmers and buyers.
  • FPOs (Farmer Producer Organisations) — collective farming and marketing.
  • Agriculture Infrastructure Fund — for post-harvest infrastructure.

Agriculture and Bihar (BPSC Focus)

Bihar's Agriculture — Key Facts

  • Bihar is one of India's most agriculture-dependent states.
  • Around 70% of the population depends on agriculture for livelihood.
  • Agriculture contributes around 18–20% of Bihar's GSDP.
  • Bihar's agriculture is characterised by:
    • Small and fragmented landholdings.
    • High population density — average landholding is very small.
    • Dependence on monsoon — irrigation coverage is limited.
    • Low mechanisation — traditional farming methods still dominate.
    • Low productivity — compared to national averages.

BPSC Must-Know: Bihar is famous for Shahi Litchi of Muzaffarpur (GI tag), Makhana of Darbhanga/Madhubani, and rice and wheat of the Ganga plains. Around 70% of Bihar's population depends on agriculture. This is the highest among major Indian states.

Bihar Agriculture — Challenges

  • Floods — recurring floods in north Bihar (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati).
  • Droughts — in south Bihar (Magadh, Chhotanagpur plateau region).
  • Small landholdings — over 90% of farmers are small and marginal.
  • Low irrigation coverage — only about 60% of the area is irrigated.
  • Low mechanisation — limited use of tractors and modern equipment.
  • Poor storage and marketing facilities — post-harvest losses.
  • Out-migration — young people leaving agriculture.
  • Fragmented supply chains — low farmer bargaining power.

Bihar Government's Agricultural Initiatives

  • Bihar Agriculture Roadmap — a comprehensive plan for agricultural growth.
  • Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali — water conservation and tree plantation.
  • Bihar Krishi Input Anudan Yojana — input subsidy for farmers.
  • Mukhyamantri Kisan Samman Nidhi — state-level income support.
  • Bihar Pashudhan Vikas Yojana — livestock development.
  • Bihar Matsya Vikas Yojana — fisheries development.
  • Bihar Rural Livelihoods Project (Jeevika) — includes agriculture and allied activities.
  • Makhana Vikas Yojana — for makhana cultivation and processing.
  • Bihar Agri-Export Policy — for export of agricultural produce.
  • Soil Health Card Scheme — implemented across Bihar.

Bihar — Agriculture and Food Security

  • Bihar has a large PDS network — with over 50,000 Fair Price Shops.
  • Under NFSA, Bihar covers a large percentage of its population — with one of the largest numbers of beneficiaries.
  • Bihar State Food Commission — monitors food security in the state.
  • Mid-Day Meal Scheme — implemented in all government schools.
  • ICDS — provides nutrition to children, pregnant women and lactating mothers.
  • Bihar has one of the highest numbers of NFSA beneficiaries in India.
  • Bihar's Public Distribution System (PDS) has been reformed with digitisation, Aadhaar linkage, and DBT for improved delivery.
  • Bihar's "Mission Kochai" — a nutrition mission for maternal and child health.
  • Bihar's "Lok Samvardhan Pariyojana" — for the upliftment of Dalits through agriculture.
BPSC Must-Know: Bihar has one of the largest number of NFSA beneficiaries in India. The state has over 50,000 Fair Price Shops. Key state schemes include the Bihar Agriculture Roadmap, Mukhyamantri Kisan Samman Nidhi, Jeevika, and Makhana Vikas Yojana. Bihar's Shahi Litchi (Muzaffarpur) and Makhana (Darbhanga) have GI tags. These are frequent BPSC questions.
