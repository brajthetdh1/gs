Maurya Vansh: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 12 min read · BPSC SSC

The Maurya Empire (321 BCE – 185 BCE) was the first pan-Indian empire in history. Founded by Chandragupta Maurya with the guidance of Chanakya (Kautilya), it reached its peak under Ashoka the Great. For BPSC and SSC, this topic is high-yield — especially the Bihar connection (Pataliputra, Barabar Caves, Ashokan pillars).

Exam Tip: Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador of Seleucus Nicator at Chandragupta's court. His account Indica is a key source for Mauryan society and administration.

Chandragupta Maurya (321–297 BCE)

Founder of the Maurya dynasty. He overthrew the Nanda ruler Dhana Nanda with the help of Chanakya and established the empire with its capital at Pataliputra (modern Patna, Bihar).

  • Defeated Seleucus Nicator in 305 BCE — gained territories up to Afghanistan.
  • Seleucus sent Megasthenes as ambassador to his court.
  • Married Helena (Seleucus's daughter) as part of a treaty.
  • Later embraced Jainism; according to tradition, died at Shravanabelagola (Karnataka) by Sallekhana.

Bindusara (297–273 BCE)

  • Son of Chandragupta; called Amitrochates ("Slayer of enemies") by the Greeks.
  • Maintained diplomatic ties with Greek kingdoms — Deimachus was a Greek envoy at his court.
  • Patronised the Ajivika sect.
  • Also known as Bhadrasara in some texts; Ashoka was his son.

Ashoka the Great (268–232 BCE)

The most famous Mauryan ruler and one of the greatest kings in world history. His reign is divided into two phases — before and after the Kalinga War (261 BCE).

Kalinga War & Change of Heart

The Kalinga war (modern Odisha) caused immense bloodshed. Ashoka, deeply moved by the suffering, renounced conquest by war and embraced Buddhism. He adopted the policy of Dhamma — a moral code based on compassion, tolerance, and non-violence.

Ashoka's Dhamma

  • Respect for elders, parents and teachers
  • Ahimsa (non-violence) and compassion towards all living beings
  • Tolerance towards all religions
  • Truthfulness, charity and humane treatment of servants

Ashokan Edicts & Inscriptions

TypeDescription

Remember: The Sarnath Lion Capital (four lions back-to-back) is the National Emblem of India. The Dhamma Chakra (Ashoka Chakra) is on our National Flag.

Mauryan Administration

  • Centralised monarchy — king was the supreme authority assisted by a council of ministers (Mantriparishad).
  • Adhyakshas — superintendents for various departments (as per Arthashastra).
  • Mahamatras — high officials managing administration.
  • Rajukas — district-level officers, especially under Ashoka.
  • Pradeshika & Yuktas — provincial and lower-level officials.
  • Empire divided into provinces, governed by princes (Kumara) or governors.
  • Spy system was well developed — mentioned in Arthashastra.

Economy & Society

  • Agriculture was the backbone — state regulated irrigation and land revenue (1/6th of produce, as per Arthashastra).
  • Punch-marked silver coins were in circulation.
  • Trade flourished — both internal and with the Hellenistic world.
  • Crafts and guilds (Shrenis) played a major role.
  • Society was divided on the basis of Varna, though Buddhism and Jainism provided alternatives.

Art & Architecture

  • Mauryan polish — a distinctive mirror-like finish on pillars (e.g. Sarnath, Lauriya-Nandangarh).
  • Stupas — Sanchi, Bharhut, Sarnath, Amaravati (Sanchi Stupa originally built by Ashoka).
  • Rock-cut caves — Barabar Caves (Bihar) — the oldest rock-cut caves in India.
  • Pottery — Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW).
  • Sculpture — Yaksha and Yakshi figures (e.g. Didarganj Yakshi).

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

  • Pataliputra (modern Patna) was the capital of the Maurya Empire — the largest city of its time.
  • Kumhrar (Patna) — archaeological site of the Mauryan palace; Ashoka's pillared hall.
  • Barabar Caves (Jehanabad district, Bihar) — oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India, dedicated by Ashoka to the Ajivikas.
  • Lauriya-Nandangarh and Lauriya-Areraj (West Champaran, Bihar) — Ashokan pillars.
  • Bulandibagh (Patna) — ancient Mauryan wooden palisade remains.
  • Bihar Museum and Patna Museum display several Mauryan artefacts.
BPSC Must-Know: Barabar Caves are in Bihar and are the oldest rock-cut caves in India. The Ashokan pillar at Lauriya-Nandangarh is one of the best-preserved pillars with the Mauryan polish.

Decline of the Mauryan Empire

  • Weak successors after Ashoka — no able ruler could hold the empire together.
  • Vast empire became difficult to administer with limited communication.
  • Brahmanical revival against Ashoka's pro-Buddhist policies.
  • Economic crisis — debasement of coins and decline in trade.
  • Provincial revolts and foreign invasions (Greek incursions from the northwest).
  • Last ruler Brihadratha was killed by his commander Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BCE, founding the Shunga dynasty.
