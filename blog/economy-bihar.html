Bihar Economy — BPSC Special: Complete Guide

15 Oct 2026 · 15 min read · BPSC SSC

This is the final post in our Economy series — a comprehensive, BPSC-focused deep dive into the Bihar Economy. As a state that is largely agriculture-dependent, faces significant development challenges, and has made remarkable progress in recent years, Bihar's economy is one of the most important topics in the BPSC syllabus. This post consolidates everything you need to know — GSDP, agriculture, industry, human development, key state schemes, and Bihar-specific economic facts. Combined with the earlier Economy posts, this gives you a complete, exam-ready preparation for BPSC.

Bihar's Economic Structure

Key Insight: Bihar's economy shows a structural anomaly — the primary sector employs ~70% of the population but contributes only ~20% to GSDP. This reflects low agricultural productivity and disguised unemployment. The services sector has grown rapidly and now contributes over half of GSDP.

Bihar's GSDP

  • Bihar's GSDP has grown rapidly in recent years — with growth rates often exceeding the national average.
  • In 2023–24, Bihar's GSDP was around ₹8.5 lakh crore (current prices).
  • Bihar's economy is one of the fastest-growing among Indian states in recent years.
  • However, in absolute terms, Bihar's GSDP is still lower than many states — due to a low starting base.

Per Capita Income

  • Bihar's per capita income is one of the lowest in India — around ₹50,000–60,000 (as of recent estimates).
  • This is significantly below the national average — indicating the state's development challenge.
  • However, per capita income has grown steadily in recent years.
  • Remittances from Bihari migrants boost per capita income significantly.
BPSC Must-Know: Bihar's per capita income is one of the lowest in India — around ₹50,000–60,000. But the state's GSDP growth has been among the fastest in recent years. This paradox — high growth, low per capita income — is a key feature of Bihar's economy.

Agriculture in Bihar

Importance of Agriculture

  • Around 70% of Bihar's population depends on agriculture for livelihood — the highest among major Indian states.
  • Agriculture contributes about 20% of Bihar's GSDP.
  • Bihar's agriculture is dominated by small and marginal farmers — over 90% of farmers have less than 2 hectares of land.
  • Average landholding in Bihar is less than 0.4 hectares — among the smallest in India.
  • Bihar's agriculture is largely rain-fed — with irrigation coverage of around 60%.
  • Bihar is among the largest producers of several crops — litchi, makhana, maize, and vegetables.

  • Mithila Makhana — GI tag — Darbhanga, Madhubani region.

BPSC Must-Know: Bihar's GI-tagged agricultural products are frequently asked in BPSC. Remember: Shahi Litchi (Muzaffarpur), Makhana (Darbhanga/Mithila), Jardalu Mango (Bhagalpur), Magahi Paan, Katarni Rice (Bhagalpur), Bhagalpur Silk, Madhubani Paintings. Around 70% of Bihar's population depends on agriculture — the highest among major Indian states.

Agricultural Challenges in Bihar

  • Floods — recurring floods in north Bihar (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati, Burhi Gandak).
  • Droughts — in south Bihar (Magadh, Chhotanagpur plateau region).
  • Small landholdings — over 90% of farmers are small and marginal.
  • Low irrigation coverage — only about 60% of the area is irrigated.
  • Low mechanisation — limited use of tractors and modern equipment.
  • Poor storage and marketing facilities — post-harvest losses.
  • Fragmented supply chains — low farmer bargaining power.
  • Out-migration — young people leaving agriculture.
  • Low agricultural productivity — compared to national averages.

Bihar's Agricultural Initiatives

  • Bihar Agriculture Roadmap — a comprehensive plan for agricultural growth.
  • Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali — water conservation and tree plantation.
  • Bihar Krishi Input Anudan Yojana — input subsidy for farmers.
  • Mukhyamantri Kisan Samman Nidhi — state-level income support.
  • Makhana Vikas Yojana — for makhana cultivation and processing.
  • Bihar Pashudhan Vikas Yojana — livestock development.
  • Bihar Matsya Vikas Yojana — fisheries development.
  • Bihar Agri-Export Policy — for export of agricultural produce.
  • Soil Health Card Scheme — implemented across Bihar.
  • PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana — irrigation expansion.

Industry in Bihar

Industrial Landscape

  • Bihar is one of India's least industrialised states.
  • Industrial contribution to GSDP is around 20–25% — lower than the national average.
  • Bihar has limited large-scale industries — most industries are small and medium.
  • The bifurcation of Bihar in 2000 — with the creation of Jharkhand — took away most mineral resources and heavy industries.
  • Bihar's industrial base was historically built around:
    • Barauni Refinery (Indian Oil — 1964).
    • Barauni Thermal Power Station.
    • Bharat Wagon and Engineering (Muzaffarpur).
    • Hindustan Fertilizer Corporation (Barauni).
    • Sugar mills in north Bihar.
    • Jute mills in Purnia and Katihar.
  • Many of these have closed or are operating below capacity.

Bihar's Industrial Strengths

  • Food Processing — high potential in litchi, makhana, maize, sugarcane, and fruits.
  • Agro-based Industries — sugar, jute, edible oils, dairy.
  • Leather Industry — with potential for exports.
  • Handloom and Handicrafts — Bhagalpur silk, Madhubani paintings.
  • Tourism — Buddhist circuit (Bodh Gaya, Nalanda, Rajgir, Vaishali).
  • IT and Services — growing sector in Patna and other cities.

Bihar's Industrial Initiatives

  • Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Policy — to attract industrial investment.
  • Bihar Startup Policy — to promote startups and entrepreneurship.
  • Bihar Industrial Area Development Authority (BIADA) — for industrial infrastructure.
  • Bihar State Investment Promotion Board (BSIPB) — for investment facilitation.
  • Bihar Industrial Policy, 2016 — incentives for new industries.
  • Bihar Food Processing Policy — for the food processing sector.
  • Bihar Textile and Leather Policy — for textile and leather industries.
  • Bihar Electronics Policy — to promote electronics manufacturing.
  • Bihar Ethanol Production Promotion Policy — for ethanol production.
  • Bihar Agri-Export Policy — for export of agricultural produce.

Bihar Industrial Corridor

  • Bihar is part of the Amritsar-Kolkata Industrial Corridor (AKIC).
  • Industrial nodes planned in Bihar — including the Buxar-Bhojpur industrial node.
  • The corridor is expected to boost industrial development in Bihar — but progress has been slow.
BPSC Must-Know: Bihar is part of the Amritsar-Kolkata Industrial Corridor (AKIC). The state's industrialisation was severely affected by the bifurcation of Bihar and Jharkhand in 2000 — most mineral and industrial resources went to Jharkhand. BIADA and BSIPB are key industrial bodies. Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Policy and Bihar Startup Policy are flagship state initiatives.

Banking & Finance in Bihar

Banking Infrastructure

  • Bihar has a lower banking density compared to the national average.
  • The Credit-Deposit (CD) Ratio in Bihar is one of the lowest in India — indicating that deposits from Bihar are not fully utilised for credit within the state.
  • The Bihar State Cooperative Bank is the apex cooperative bank in the state.
  • Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) operating in Bihar:
    • Madhya Bihar Gramin Bank (sponsored by Punjab National Bank).
    • Dakshin Bihar Gramin Bank (sponsored by Punjab National Bank).
    • Uttar Bihar Gramin Bank (sponsored by Central Bank of India).
  • Public Sector Banks have a strong presence in Bihar — especially SBI, PNB, Canara Bank, and Bank of Baroda.

Financial Inclusion in Bihar

  • PMJDY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana) — implemented extensively in Bihar.
  • Jeevika — promotes financial inclusion through women's self-help groups (SHGs).
  • MUDRA Yojana — provides loans to small businesses across Bihar.
  • Stand Up India — for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs.
  • Digital payments — UPI and mobile banking increasingly used.
  • Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) — improved efficiency in welfare delivery.

Bihar's Fiscal Position

Bihar Budget

  • Bihar's budget is presented annually in the Bihar Vidhan Sabha by the Finance Minister of Bihar.
  • Presented under Article 202 of the Constitution.
  • Key focus areas: agriculture, education, health, infrastructure, and welfare schemes.

Bihar FRBM Act

  • Bihar has its own Bihar Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act — enacted in 2006.
  • Aimed at prudent fiscal management, reducing fiscal deficit, and improving transparency.
  • Bihar's fiscal deficit has remained within FRBM targets in recent years.

Tax Revenue

  • Bihar's own tax revenue is one of the lowest among Indian states — reflecting the state's low tax base.
  • Key sources of Bihar's tax revenue:
    • SGST — State Goods and Services Tax.
    • State Excise Duty — on alcohol (impacted by prohibition).
    • Stamp Duty and Registration Fees.
    • Motor Vehicle Tax.
  • Bihar is highly dependent on central transfers — through the Finance Commission and centrally sponsored schemes.

Prohibition and Its Impact

  • Bihar imposed total prohibition on alcohol in April 2016.
  • Before prohibition, state excise duty on alcohol was a significant source of revenue (over ₹3,000 crore per year).
  • After prohibition, this revenue was lost.
  • However, prohibition has generated social benefits — improved health, reduced domestic violence, higher school attendance.
  • This is an example of the trade-off between social objectives (Article 47 of DPSP) and tax revenue.

Human Development in Bihar

HDI Position

  • Bihar has historically ranked low on the HDI — among the bottom states in India.
  • However, Bihar's HDI has improved significantly in recent years.
  • Key improvements:
    • Life expectancy has increased.
    • Infant mortality has declined.
    • Literacy rate has improved — from 47% (2001) to 61.8% (2011) — and further in recent years.
    • School enrolment has increased.
    • Poverty has declined.
  • Key challenges:
    • Low per capita income.
    • High dropout rates in schools.
    • Poor health indicators in rural areas.
    • Low female labour force participation.
    • High out-migration of young workers.

Poverty in Bihar

  • According to the Tendulkar Committee (2011–12), Bihar's poverty ratio was around 33.7% — one of the highest among major states.
  • Bihar's poverty has declined significantly in recent decades — but remains above the national average.
  • Bihar's Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) has declined sharply — one of the fastest reductions in India.
  • Key dimensions of poverty in Bihar:
    • Low per capita income.
    • Low literacy and education levels.
    • Limited health infrastructure.
    • Dependence on agriculture.
    • Recurring floods and droughts.
    • High population density.

Unemployment in Bihar

  • Bihar has one of the highest unemployment rates among Indian states — especially among youth.
  • Educated unemployment is a serious challenge — lakhs of graduates and post-graduates are unemployed.
  • Disguised unemployment in agriculture is very high.
  • Seasonal unemployment is common — agriculture provides work only for a few months.
  • Out-migration is very high — lakhs of Bihari workers migrate to other states and abroad for work.
  • Remittances from migrants are a major source of income for Bihar.
BPSC Must-Know: Bihar has one of the highest unemployment rates among Indian states — especially educated unemployment. Out-migration from Bihar is very high — remittances are a major source of income. Bihar's HDI and per capita income remain below the national average — but have improved significantly in recent years.

Bihar — Key Government Schemes

Saat Nishchay (Seven Resolves)

The flagship programme of the Bihar Government — launched in 2015 — covering 7 key areas:

  1. Aarthik Hal, Yuvaon Ko Bal — Economic empowerment of youth.
  2. Aarakshit Rozgar Mahilaon Ka Adhikar — Reserved employment for women.
  3. Har Ghar Bijli — Electricity for every household.
  4. Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal — Piped drinking water to every household.
  5. Ghar Tak Pakki Gali-Naliyan — Paved lanes and drains up to every house.
  6. Shauchalay Nirman Ghar-Ghar — Toilets in every household.
  7. Awas Samaan — Housing for all.

Bihar's Development Challenges

  1. Low per capita income — among the lowest in India.
  2. High population density — creates pressure on resources.
  3. Recurring floods and droughts — affecting agriculture and infrastructure.
  4. Low industrialisation — limits non-farm employment.
  5. Low credit-deposit ratio — limits investment.
  6. Low human development — despite improvements.
  7. Large-scale out-migration — of skilled workers.
  8. Low agricultural productivity — compared to national averages.
  9. Poor infrastructure — roads, power, warehouses, cold chains.
  10. Low female labour force participation.
  11. Small and fragmented landholdings.
  12. High poverty levels — despite recent reductions.

Bihar's Development Opportunities

  1. Demographic dividend — young population.
  2. Agri-export potential — litchi, makhana, maize, fruits, vegetables.
  3. Food processing — high value addition.
  4. Handloom and handicrafts — Bhagalpur silk, Madhubani paintings.
  5. Inland waterways — National Waterway-1 on the Ganga.
  6. Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor — improves freight movement.
  7. Amritsar-Kolkata Industrial Corridor — industrial development.
  8. Tourism — Buddhist circuit — attracts international visitors.
  9. IT and services — growing sector in Patna and other cities.
  10. Renewable energy — solar and biomass potential.
  11. Education and skill development — improving workforce quality.
  12. Remittances — supporting household consumption.
