State Government — Governor, CM & Legislature: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 15 min read · BPSC SSC

The State Government in India mirrors the Union Government — with a Governor as the constitutional head, a Chief Minister as the real executive, and a State Legislature that makes laws for the state. The Governor is appointed by the President, while the Chief Minister is the leader of the majority party in the Vidhan Sabha. The State Legislature can be unicameral (only Vidhan Sabha) or bicameral (Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad) — and Bihar has a bicameral legislature. For BPSC and SSC, this topic is extremely high-yield — especially the Governor's discretionary powers, the CM's role, and Bihar-specific facts.

State Government — Composition

  • Governor — Article 153–162 (constitutional head of the state).
  • Chief Minister — Article 163–164 (real executive head).
  • Council of Ministers — Article 163–164 (headed by the CM).
  • State Legislature — Article 168–212 (Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad, if bicameral).
  • Advocate General of the State — Article 165 (first law officer of the state).
  • State Public Service Commission — Article 315 (for recruitment to state services).
  • State Finance Commission — Article 243-I (for panchayat finances).
Exam Tip: The Governor is the constitutional head of the state — but the real executive power lies with the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers. This mirrors the Union Government's structure (President vs PM).

THE GOVERNOR — Article 153 to 162

Appointment (Article 155)

  • The Governor is appointed by the President — not elected.
  • He holds office during the pleasure of the President — no fixed tenure in practice.
  • Usually, the Governor is appointed for a term of 5 years — but can be removed earlier by the President.
  • There is no fixed qualification laid down in the Constitution — but by convention, the Governor should be:
    • A citizen of India.
    • At least 35 years of age.
    • Not a member of any legislature.
    • Not holding any office of profit.
  • In practice, the Governor is appointed by the President on the advice of the Union Council of Ministers.
  • There is no bar on the appointment of a non-resident of the state as Governor.

Term and Removal

  • Holds office for 5 years — but can be removed earlier by the President.
  • The Governor can resign by writing to the President.
  • The Governor continues in office until the successor takes charge.
  • The Governor can be transferred from one state to another by the President.
  • There is no fixed procedure for removal — it is purely at the President's discretion.
Remember: The Governor is appointed by the President — not elected. He holds office for 5 years but serves during the pleasure of the President. There is no impeachment procedure for the Governor — unlike the President.

Powers of the Governor

Executive Powers

  • All executive actions of the State Government are taken in the Governor's name.
  • Appoints the Chief Minister and, on his advice, other ministers.
  • Appoints the Advocate General of the state.
  • Appoints members of the State Public Service Commission.
  • Appoints the Chairperson and members of the State Finance Commission.
  • Can appoint commissions to investigate matters of state importance.
  • Administers the Union Territories if appointed as Administrator (e.g. Delhi, Puducherry).

Legislative Powers

  • Summons and prorogues the State Legislature.
  • Can dissolve the Vidhan Sabha (on the advice of the CM).
  • Addresses the State Legislature at the commencement of the first session after each general election.
  • Nominates 1/6 of the members of the Vidhan Parishad — from literature, science, art, cooperative movement, and social service (in bicameral states).
  • In bicameral states, the Governor nominates some members to the Vidhan Parishad.
  • Gives assent to bills passed by the State Legislature — or withholds it, or returns them for reconsideration (except Money Bills).
  • Can promulgate Ordinances when the State Legislature is not in session (Article 213).
  • Has the power of veto — like the President.

Financial Powers

  • No Money Bill can be introduced in the State Legislature without the Governor's recommendation.
  • The State Budget is presented in the Governor's name.
  • The Contingency Fund of the State is at the disposal of the Governor.
  • Appoints the State Finance Commission every 5 years.

Judicial Powers

  • Appoints the Advocate General of the state.
  • Has the power to pardon, reprieve, respite, remit or commute sentences — but only in cases relating to state laws (Article 161).
    • Cannot pardon a death sentence — that is the President's exclusive power under Article 72.
    • Cannot pardon sentences by court-martial.
  • Can seek the advisory opinion of the High Court on questions of law (Article 143 applies to the Supreme Court, but the Governor can also refer certain matters).

Emergency Powers

  • Cannot declare an emergency — but the President can impose President's Rule under Article 356.
  • Recommends President's Rule to the President when there is a failure of constitutional machinery in the state.
BPSC Must-Know: The Governor's judicial power under Article 161 is limited — he can pardon, reprieve, remit or commute sentences for offences against state laws, but cannot pardon a death sentence (that is the President's exclusive power under Article 72).

Discretionary Powers of the Governor

These are the most important powers of the Governor — because they are exercised without the advice of the Council of Ministers.

  • Appointment of the Chief Minister — when no party has a clear majority.
  • Dismissal of the Council of Ministers — when it loses the confidence of the Vidhan Sabha.
  • Dissolution of the Vidhan Sabha — when the government has lost its majority.
  • Recommending President's Rule — when there is a breakdown of constitutional machinery.
  • Reserving a bill for the President's consideration — when the bill is against the Constitution or the interests of the state.
  • Returning a bill for reconsideration by the State Legislature.
  • Seeking information from the Chief Minister on state affairs.
  • Special responsibilities — in some states (e.g. Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, etc.) for specific matters like law and order, tribal welfare.

Constitutional Discretion vs Situational Discretion

  • Constitutional discretion — expressly provided by the Constitution (e.g. appointment of CM in a hung assembly).
  • Situational discretion — emerges from a particular situation (e.g. dismissing a government that has lost majority).

THE CHIEF MINISTER — Article 163 to 164

Appointment (Article 164)

  • The Governor appoints the Chief Minister.
  • Usually, the leader of the majority party in the Vidhan Sabha is appointed as CM.
  • If no party has a clear majority, the Governor may invite the leader most likely to command a majority — or a coalition leader.
  • The CM must be a member of the State Legislature — and if not, must become a member within 6 months of appointment.

Term of Office

  • No fixed term — holds office during the pleasure of the Governor — but in practice, means as long as he enjoys the confidence of the Vidhan Sabha.
  • Can be removed by a vote of no-confidence in the Vidhan Sabha.
  • Can resign by submitting his resignation to the Governor.

Powers and Functions of the Chief Minister

  • Head of the State Government — the real executive authority.
  • Leader of the Council of Ministers — recommends ministers for appointment; allocates portfolios; can ask a minister to resign.
  • Chief adviser to the Governor — communicates decisions of the Council to the Governor.
  • Leader of the Vidhan Sabha — guides the legislative programme of the government.
  • Chairman of the State Planning Board (or NITI Aayog's state counterpart).
  • Chief spokesperson of the state government.
  • Crisis manager — coordinates response during emergencies.
  • Represents the state at inter-state meetings and central-state consultations.
Exam Tip: The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor, but must have the confidence of the Vidhan Sabha. The CM is the real executive head of the state — the Governor is only the nominal head.

STATE COUNCIL OF MINISTERS — Article 163

Composition

  • Headed by the Chief Minister.
  • Consists of Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, and Deputy Ministers.
  • The total number of ministers — including the CM — shall not exceed 15% of the total number of members of the Vidhan Sabha (91st Amendment, 2003).
  • A minister must be a member of either House of the State Legislature — or become one within 6 months of appointment.

Functions

  • Aid and advise the Governor in the exercise of his functions.
  • Formulate and implement the policies of the state government.
  • Introduce bills in the State Legislature and guide their passage.
  • Manage the administration of the state.
  • Prepare the state budget.
  • Collectively responsible to the Vidhan Sabha.

Collective Responsibility

  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Vidhan Sabha.
  • A vote of no-confidence against the Council leads to its resignation.
  • Individual ministers are personally responsible to the Governor for their ministry's work.

STATE LEGISLATURE — Article 168

Composition

  • The State Legislature consists of the Governor and one or two Houses.
  • If unicameral — only the Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly).
  • If bicameral — the Vidhan Sabha and the Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council).
  • Currently, 6 states have bicameral legislatures: Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana.
  • Article 169 provides for the creation or abolition of the Vidhan Parishad by Parliament on the recommendation of the State Legislative Assembly.

Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly)

  • Maximum strength: 500 members; minimum: 60 members (exceptions for smaller states like Sikkim, Goa, etc.).
  • Members are elected directly by the people from territorial constituencies on the basis of universal adult suffrage.
  • Term: 5 years — but can be dissolved earlier by the Governor.
  • During a National Emergency, its term can be extended by one year at a time — not beyond 6 months after the Emergency ends.
  • The Speaker and Deputy Speaker are elected by the members.
  • The Speaker presides over the sessions and has a casting vote in case of a tie.
  • Seats are reserved for SCs and STs on the basis of population.

Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council)

  • Maximum strength: 1/3 of the strength of the Vidhan Sabha; minimum: 40 members (exceptions for Jammu & Kashmir, now a UT).
  • Members are elected indirectly — through a complex system:
    • 1/3 by members of local bodies (municipalities, panchayats).
    • 1/3 by members of the Vidhan Sabha.
    • 1/12 by graduates of 3 years' standing.
    • 1/12 by teachers of secondary schools and above.
    • 1/6 nominated by the Governor (literature, science, art, cooperative movement, social service).
  • Term: The Vidhan Parishad is a permanent house — cannot be dissolved.
  • Members serve a term of 6 years — one-third retire every 2 years.
  • The Chairman and Deputy Chairman preside over the sessions.
  • The Vidhan Parishad has limited powers — it can only delay ordinary bills (not reject them).
  • Money Bills can be introduced only in the Vidhan Sabha — the Vidhan Parishad can only recommend changes within 14 days.

BPSC Must-Know: Bihar has a bicameral legislature — Vidhan Sabha (243) and Vidhan Parishad (75). State Legislature — Powers and Functions

Legislative Powers

  • Makes laws on subjects in the State List and Concurrent List.
  • Can make laws on Union List subjects under special circumstances (Article 249, 250, 252, 253).
  • Can amend the Constitution in matters relating to the state (with ratification).

Financial Powers

  • Approves the State Budget.
  • Approves taxation proposals.
  • Controls the State Consolidated Fund.
  • Money Bills can be introduced only in the Vidhan Sabha.

Executive Control

  • Question Hour — members question ministers.
  • No-Confidence Motion — can remove the state government.
  • Censure Motion — expresses disapproval of a policy.
  • Adjournment Motion — draws attention to urgent matters.
  • Committees — examine the work of the executive.

Judicial Powers

  • Can remove the Speaker and Deputy Speaker.
  • Can punish members for breach of privilege.
  • Can decide on the disqualification of members.

Electoral Powers

  • Participates in the election of the President (elected members only).
  • Elected MLAs participate in the election of Rajya Sabha members from the state.

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

Bihar Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)

  • Bihar Vidhan Sabha has 243 seats — 191 general, 38 reserved for SCs, 2 for STs.
  • The Speaker presides over the Vidhan Sabha; the Deputy Speaker assists him.
  • Members are elected directly by the people of Bihar.
  • Term: 5 years (unless dissolved earlier).

Bihar Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)

  • Bihar Vidhan Parishad has 75 members — one of the largest in India.
  • The Vidhan Parishad is a permanent house — cannot be dissolved.
  • 1/3 of members retire every 2 years; each member serves a 6-year term.
  • The Chairman presides over the Vidhan Parishad.

First Chief Minister of Bihar

  • Shri Krishna Sinha (also known as Sri Babu) — the first Chief Minister of Bihar (1937–1961).
  • He was the longest-serving Chief Minister of Bihar — served for 24 years (with brief interruptions).
  • He was a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi and a prominent leader of the Indian National Congress.
  • His tenure saw the abolition of the zamindari system in Bihar — a landmark reform.
  • He was succeeded by Binodanand Jha in 1961.

Notable Chief Ministers of Bihar

  • Shri Krishna Sinha — first CM (1937–1961).
  • Karpoori Thakur — CM (1970–71, 1977–80); implemented OBC reservation in Bihar — a landmark in social justice.
  • Lalu Prasad Yadav — CM (1990–1997); champion of backward caste politics.
  • Nitish Kumar — longest-serving CM after Shri Krishna Sinha; multiple terms (2005–2014, 2015–2017, 2017–2020, 2020–2024, 2024–present).

Governors of Bihar — Notable

  • Sir James David Sifton — first Governor of Bihar (1936–1937).
  • Shri R.R. Diwakar — first Indian Governor of Bihar (1947–1952).
  • Dr. Zakir Husain — Governor of Bihar (1957–1962); later became the third President of India.
  • Ram Nath Kovind — Governor of Bihar (2015–2017); later became the 14th President of India.
  • Phagu Chauhan — Governor of Bihar (2019–2023).
  • Rajendra Vishwanath Arlekar — Governor of Bihar (2023–present).

Bihar — Key Political Facts

  • Bihar has 40 Lok Sabha seats and 16 Rajya Sabha seats.
  • Bihar Vidhan Sabha — 243 seats (191 general, 38 SC, 2 ST).
  • Bihar Vidhan Parishad — 75 seats (one of the largest in India).
  • Patna High Court — established 1 March 1916; one of the oldest in India.
  • Bihar is one of 6 states with a bicameral legislature.
  • Bihar was the first state to implement 50% reservation for women in Panchayati Raj institutions (2006).
  • Bihar Diwas — celebrated on 22 March (the day Bihar became a separate province in 1912).
BPSC Must-Know: Shri Krishna Sinha (Sri Babu) was the first Chief Minister of Bihar and the longest-serving CM. Bihar has 243 Vidhan Sabha seats and 75 Vidhan Parishad seats — making it one of 6 bicameral states in India. Karpoori Thakur's OBC reservation (1970s) is a landmark in Bihar's political history.
