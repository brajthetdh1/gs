Union Executive — President, PM & Council of Ministers: Complete Guide

15 Oct 2026 · 15 min read · BPSC SSC

The Union Executive consists of the President, the Vice-President, the Prime Minister, and the Council of Ministers. These are the key institutions that run the Government of India. While the President is the constitutional head of state, real executive power is exercised by the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers. For BPSC and SSC, this topic is extremely high-yield — especially the election and powers of the President, the appointment of the PM, and the role of the Council of Ministers.

Union Executive — Composition

  • President — Article 52–62 (constitutional head of state).
  • Vice-President — Article 63–71 (second-highest office).
  • Prime Minister — Article 74–75 (head of government).
  • Council of Ministers — Article 74 (headed by the PM).
  • Attorney General of India — Article 76 (first law officer).
  • Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) — Article 148 (independent constitutional body).
Exam Tip: The President is the nominal or constitutional executive — the real executive is the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers. This reflects the British parliamentary system. Article 74 provides that the President shall act on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

THE PRESIDENT — Article 52 to 62

Qualification (Article 58)

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must have completed 35 years of age.
  • Must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha.
  • Must not hold any office of profit under the Government of India or of any State (exceptions: President, Vice-President, Governor, Minister, etc.).

Election of the President (Article 54–55)

  • Elected by an Electoral College consisting of:
    • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha).
    • Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States (including Delhi and Puducherry).
  • Nominated members (e.g. nominated members of Rajya Sabha) do not participate.
  • Members of Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishads) do not participate.
  • Election is by secret ballot — using the proportional representation system by means of a single transferable vote.
  • System of proportional representation ensures uniformity in the scale of representation of different States — and equality between the Union and the States as a whole.

Term of Office (Article 56)

  • Holds office for a term of 5 years from the date of entering office.
  • Can be re-elected — no limit on the number of terms.
  • Can resign by writing to the Vice-President.
  • Can be removed by impeachment (Article 61).
  • Continues in office until the successor takes charge.

Impeachment of the President (Article 61)

  • Grounds: Violation of the Constitution.
  • Can be initiated in either House of Parliament.
  • A resolution must be passed by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House initiating the impeachment.
  • The other House investigates the charges.
  • The President has the right to appear and be represented in the investigation.
  • If the other House also passes the resolution by a two-thirds majority of total membership, the President stands removed.
  • Note: No President has been impeached in India so far.

Vacancy in the Office of the President

  • Vacancy can occur by death, resignation, removal, or otherwise.
  • If vacancy occurs, the Vice-President acts as President (Article 65).
  • If the Vice-President is unavailable, the Chief Justice of India — or in his absence, the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court — acts as President.
  • Election to fill the vacancy must be held within 6 months.
  • The acting President cannot exercise certain powers — like the power to dissolve the Lok Sabha.
Remember: The President is elected indirectly by an Electoral College using the single transferable vote system of proportional representation. The Vice-President acts as President during a vacancy. No President has ever been impeached in India.

Powers of the President

Executive Powers

  • All executive actions of the Government of India are taken in the name of the President (Article 77).
  • Appoints the Prime Minister and, on his advice, other ministers.
  • Appoints the Governors of States, Attorney General, CAG, Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners, UPSC Chairman and members, Finance Commission, Chief Justice and other judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Appoints the Chief of the Army, Navy and Air Force.
  • Appoints ambassadors and high commissioners to other countries.
  • Administers Union Territories through Chief Commissioners or Administrators.
  • Has the power to appoint a commission to investigate conditions of SCs, STs, and other backward classes.

Legislative Powers

  • Summons and prorogues both Houses of Parliament.
  • Can dissolve the Lok Sabha (on the advice of the Council of Ministers).
  • Addresses both Houses at the commencement of the first session after each general election — and the first session of each year (Article 87).
  • Nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha (from fields of literature, science, art and social service).
  • Nominates 2 members to the Lok Sabha from the Anglo-Indian community (this provision lapsed in 2020).
  • Gives assent to bills passed by Parliament — or withholds it, or returns the bill for reconsideration (except Money Bills and Constitutional Amendment Bills).
  • Can promulgate Ordinances when Parliament is not in session (Article 123).
  • Has the power of veto — absolute, suspensive, and pocket veto.

Financial Powers

  • No Money Bill can be introduced in Parliament without the President's recommendation.
  • The annual Union Budget is presented to Parliament in the President's name.
  • The Contingency Fund of India is at the disposal of the President.
  • Appoints the Finance Commission every 5 years.

Judicial Powers

  • Appoints the Chief Justice and judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Can pardon, reprieve, respite, remit or commute sentences (Article 72):
    • In all cases of death sentence.
    • In all cases of punishment by court-martial.
    • In all cases where the punishment is for offences against laws relating to matters to which the executive power of the Union extends.
  • Can seek the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court (Article 143).

Emergency Powers

  • National Emergency — Article 352.
  • President's Rule (State Emergency) — Article 356.
  • Financial Emergency — Article 360.

Military Powers

  • Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces of India.
  • Can declare war or peace — subject to parliamentary approval.
  • Appoints the Chiefs of the three armed forces.

Diplomatic Powers

  • Represents India in international affairs.
  • Appoints ambassadors and high commissioners.
  • Receives credentials of foreign diplomats.
  • Signs international treaties and agreements — subject to parliamentary approval.
BPSC Must-Know: The President's veto power has three forms — absolute veto (withhold assent), suspensive veto (return for reconsideration), and pocket veto (keep the bill pending indefinitely). The President has no veto over Constitutional Amendment Bills.

THE VICE-PRESIDENT — Article 63 to 71

Qualification (Article 66)

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must have completed 35 years of age.
  • Must be qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Must not hold any office of profit under the Government of India or of any State.

Election of the Vice-President

  • Elected by an Electoral College consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament (elected and nominated).
  • State Legislature members do not participate — unlike in the President's election.
  • Election is by secret ballot — using the proportional representation system by means of a single transferable vote.

Term of Office

  • Holds office for 5 years.
  • Can be re-elected — no limit on the number of terms.
  • Resigns by writing to the President.
  • Can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha — passed by a majority of all the then members of the Rajya Sabha and agreed to by the Lok Sabha.
  • No formal impeachment — removal requires only a simple majority in the Rajya Sabha (with the Lok Sabha's concurrence).

Powers and Functions

  • Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Acts as President when the office of the President is vacant.
  • Performs the functions of the President when the President is unable to discharge his duties (illness, absence, etc.).
  • During this period, the Vice-President does not perform the duties of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Has a casting vote in the Rajya Sabha in case of a tie — like the Speaker in the Lok Sabha.
Exam Tip: The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. He is elected by both Houses of Parliament — but State Legislature members do not participate in his election. This is a favourite BPSC and SSC question.

THE PRIME MINISTER — Article 74 to 75

Appointment (Article 75)

  • The President appoints the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha as Prime Minister.
  • If no party has a clear majority, the President may invite the leader most likely to command a majority — or a coalition leader.
  • The PM is appointed by the President — but must have the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  • The PM can be a member of either House of Parliament — but if not a member of either House within 6 months of appointment, he ceases to be PM.

Term of Office

  • No fixed term — the PM holds office during the pleasure of the President — but in practice, this means as long as he enjoys the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  • The PM can be removed by a vote of no-confidence in the Lok Sabha.
  • Can resign by submitting his resignation to the President.

Powers and Functions of the Prime Minister

  • Head of the Government — the real executive authority.
  • Leader of the Council of Ministers — recommends ministers for appointment; allocates portfolios; can ask a minister to resign.
  • Chief adviser to the President — communicates decisions of the Council to the President.
  • Leader of the Lok Sabha — in most cases; guides the legislative programme of the government.
  • Chairman of the NITI Aayog, Inter-State Council, National Integration Council, etc.
  • Chief spokesperson of the government in Parliament and in the country.
  • Crisis manager — coordinates response during emergencies.
  • Represents India at international summits — G20, BRICS, etc.
Remember: The Prime Minister is the real executive head of India — the President is only the nominal head. The PM is the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha and heads the Council of Ministers.

COUNCIL OF MINISTERS — Article 74

Composition

  • Headed by the Prime Minister.
  • Consists of Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (independent charge and non-independent charge), and Deputy Ministers.
  • The total number of ministers — including the PM — shall not exceed 15% of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha (91st Amendment, 2003).
  • A minister must be a member of either House of Parliament — or become one within 6 months of appointment (91st Amendment).

Categories of Ministers

CategoryRole
Cabinet MinistersSenior ministers heading important ministries; attend Cabinet meetings
Ministers of StateJunior ministers assisting Cabinet Ministers; may have independent charge of a ministry
Deputy MinistersJunior-most ministers; assist Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State

Functions of the Council of Ministers

  • Aid and advise the President — the President acts on their advice (Article 74).
  • Formulate and implement policies of the government.
  • Introduce bills in Parliament and guide their passage.
  • Manage the administration of the Union.
  • Prepare the budget and manage the economy.
  • Represent India in international affairs.
  • Responsible to the Lok Sabha — collective responsibility.

Individual vs Collective Responsibility

  • Individual responsibility: Each minister is personally responsible to the President for the work of his ministry.
  • Collective responsibility: The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75(3)).
  • A vote of no-confidence against one minister or the entire Council leads to the resignation of the entire Council.
Exam Tip: Article 74 — the Council of Ministers aids and advises the President. Article 75 — the PM is appointed by the President; ministers are appointed on the PM's advice; the Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. The 91st Amendment (2003) capped the number of ministers at 15% of the Lok Sabha's strength.

Other Union Executive Officers

Attorney General of India (Article 76)

  • The first law officer of the Government of India.
  • Appointed by the President.
  • Must be qualified to be a judge of the Supreme Court.
  • Advises the Government of India on legal matters.
  • Appears for the Government in the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Has the right of audience in all courts in India.
  • Has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of both Houses of Parliament — but no right to vote.
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President — no fixed term.

Solicitor General of India

  • The second law officer of the Government of India.
  • Assists the Attorney General.
  • Appointed by the Government of India (not a constitutional post).

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad — born in Zeradei, Siwan district, Bihar — was the first President of India (1950–1962). He is the only President to have been elected twice, and the longest-serving President of India.
  • Before becoming President, Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly — which framed the Indian Constitution.
  • He also served as the President of the Indian National Congress (1934, 1939, 1947).
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad's birthday — 3 December — is celebrated as Dr. Rajendra Prasad Jayanti in Bihar.
  • His ancestral home at Zeradei is now maintained as a memorial.
  • Bihar has produced several Union Ministers — including Lal Bahadur Shastri's mentor Dr. Rajendra Prasad, and later leaders like Jagjivan Ram (who was Deputy PM), Lalu Prasad Yadav (Railway Minister), Nitish Kumar (Chief Minister and former Union Minister), and others.
  • Bihar is also represented in the Electoral College for the President's election — through its elected MLAs and MPs.
  • The President's rule (Article 356) has been imposed in Bihar multiple times — the most recent being in 2005 before Nitish Kumar became CM.
BPSC Must-Know: Dr. Rajendra Prasad — from Zeradei, Siwan (Bihar) — was the first President of India and the only President to serve two full terms. He was also the President of the Constituent Assembly. This is one of the most important Bihar-related facts for BPSC.
