Parliament — Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha: Complete Guide for BPSC & SSC

15 Oct 2026 · 15 min read · BPSC SSC

The Parliament of India is the supreme legislative body of the country. Under Article 79, the Parliament consists of the President, the Rajya Sabha (Council of States), and the Lok Sabha (House of the People). The Indian Parliament follows the British parliamentary model — with a head of state (President) who is the nominal authority and a Council of Ministers responsible to the lower house. For BPSC and SSC, Parliament is one of the most frequently tested topics — especially the composition of the two Houses, the Speaker's role, types of bills, and parliamentary procedures.

Composition of Parliament

Under Article 79, the Parliament consists of:

  • The President of India — not a member of either House but an integral part of Parliament.
  • The Rajya Sabha (Council of States) — the upper house.
  • The Lok Sabha (House of the People) — the lower house.
Exam Tip: The President is an integral part of Parliament even though he does not sit in either House. No bill becomes law without the President's assent. This is a favourite BPSC and SSC question.

RAJYA SABHA (Council of States)

Composition (Article 80)

  • Maximum strength: 250 members.
  • Current strength: 245 members.
  • 238 members — representatives of States and Union Territories, elected indirectly.
  • 12 members — nominated by the President from fields of literature, science, art, and social service.

Election of Rajya Sabha Members

  • Members are elected by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies (not by the public directly).
  • Election is by proportional representation by means of a single transferable vote.
  • For Union Territories, members are elected by an Electoral College specially constituted for the purpose.
  • The nominated members are appointed by the President — they have the same powers as elected members, except they cannot vote in the election of the President.

Qualification (Article 84)

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must have completed 30 years of age.
  • Must make an oath before the Election Commission.
  • Must possess other qualifications prescribed by Parliament.

Term of Office

  • The Rajya Sabha is a permanent house — it is not subject to dissolution.
  • Members serve a term of 6 years.
  • One-third of members retire every 2 years — this ensures continuity.
  • The Rajya Sabha is called the "House of Elders" and the "Second Chamber".

Presiding Officers

  • Chairman — the Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Deputy Chairman — elected by the members of the Rajya Sabha from among themselves.
  • The Chairman presides over the sessions and has a casting vote in case of a tie.
Remember: The Rajya Sabha is a permanent House — it cannot be dissolved. One-third of its members retire every two years. The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

Special Powers of the Rajya Sabha

  • Article 249 — can authorise Parliament to make laws on State List subjects by a resolution passed by a two-thirds majority.
  • Article 312 — can authorise the creation of new All India Services.
  • Can initiate the removal of the Vice-President (by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha, agreed to by the Lok Sabha).
  • Cannot be dissolved — provides continuity and acts as a revising chamber.
  • Elected members of the Rajya Sabha participate in the election of the President.

LOK SABHA (House of the People)

Composition (Article 81)

  • Maximum strength: 552 members (as per the original Constitution).
  • Currently, the Lok Sabha has 543 elected members.
  • Members are elected directly by the people from territorial constituencies on the basis of universal adult suffrage.
  • Earlier, the President nominated 2 members from the Anglo-Indian community — but this provision lapsed in 2020 (after the 104th Amendment, which extended SC/ST reservation but ended Anglo-Indian nomination).
  • Seats are reserved for SCs and STs on the basis of population.

Qualification (Article 84)

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must have completed 25 years of age.
  • Must make an oath before the Election Commission.
  • Must possess other qualifications prescribed by Parliament.

Term of Office

  • Normal term is 5 years from the date of its first sitting.
  • Can be dissolved earlier by the President on the advice of the Council of Ministers.
  • During a National Emergency, its term can be extended by one year at a time — but not beyond 6 months after the Emergency ends.
  • The Lok Sabha is called the "House of the People" and the "Lower House".

Presiding Officers

  • Speaker — elected by the members of the Lok Sabha from among themselves.
  • Deputy Speaker — also elected by the members.
  • The Speaker has a casting vote in case of a tie — but only in the first instance.
  • He can be removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the House.
  • He continues in office even after the Lok Sabha is dissolved — until the new Lok Sabha meets.
Exam Tip: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is the highest authority of the House. His decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill is final. The Speaker has the power to adjourn the House, suspend members, and decide on points of order.

Special Powers of the Lok Sabha

  • Money Bills can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
  • The Lok Sabha has the final say on Money Bills — the Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes (within 14 days).
  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75).
  • A vote of no-confidence can be passed only in the Lok Sabha.
  • The Lok Sabha has special powers over the National Emergency — its approval is needed to continue the Emergency.

Types of Bills in Parliament

1. Ordinary Bills

  • Can be introduced in either House.
  • Passed by a simple majority in both Houses.
  • If the two Houses disagree, a joint sitting can be called (Article 108).
  • Sent to the President for assent — who can give assent, withhold it, or return it.

2. Money Bills (Article 110)

  • Can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
  • Introduced only on the recommendation of the President.
  • The Speaker certifies whether a bill is a Money Bill.
  • The Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days — the Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.
  • The President can either give assent or withhold it — but cannot return a Money Bill.
  • Examples: Finance Bill, Appropriation Bill, any bill dealing with taxation, borrowing, or the Consolidated Fund.

3. Financial Bills

  • Deal with financial matters but are not Money Bills.
  • Can be introduced in either House — but only on the President's recommendation.
  • All Money Bills are Financial Bills, but not all Financial Bills are Money Bills.

4. Constitution Amendment Bills (Article 368)

  • Can be introduced in either House.
  • Requires a special majority — two-thirds of members present and voting + majority of total membership of each House.
  • Some amendments also require ratification by half the States.
  • No provision for a joint sitting for Constitutional Amendment Bills.
  • The President must give assent — he cannot return or withhold it.

5. Ordinance Replacing Bills

  • The President can promulgate an Ordinance when Parliament is not in session (Article 123).
  • The Ordinance must be approved by Parliament within 6 weeks of reassembly.
  • If not approved, the Ordinance lapses.
BPSC Must-Know: Money Bills can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha — on the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days. The Speaker certifies whether a bill is a Money Bill. The President cannot return a Money Bill.

Parliamentary Procedures

Question Hour

  • The first hour of every parliamentary sitting is devoted to questions.
  • Members ask questions of ministers on matters of public importance.
  • Three types of questions:
    • Starred Question — requires an oral answer; supplementary questions can follow.
    • Unstarred Question — requires a written answer; no supplementary questions.
    • Short Notice Question — asked with less than 10 days' notice.

Zero Hour

  • Not mentioned in the rules of procedure — an Indian innovation.
  • Starts immediately after the Question Hour.
  • Members raise urgent matters without prior notice.

Motions in Parliament

Call Attention MotionTo draw the attention of a minister to a matter of urgent public importance — Indian innovation

Sessions of Parliament

  • The President summons each House — the gap between two sessions cannot exceed 6 months.
  • Three sessions in a year:
    • Budget Session — February to May (longest).
    • Monsoon Session — July to September.
    • Winter Session — November to December.
  • Normally, Parliament meets for about 100 days in a year.

Quorum

  • The minimum number of members required to be present for a valid sitting is one-tenth of the total number of members.
  • For the Lok Sabha: 55 members (out of 543); for the Rajya Sabha: 25 members (out of 245).

Powers and Functions of Parliament

Legislative Powers

  • Makes laws on subjects in the Union List and Concurrent List.
  • Can make laws on State List subjects under special circumstances (Article 249, 250, 252, 253).
  • Can amend the Constitution (Article 368).

Financial Powers

  • Approves the Union Budget.
  • Approves taxation proposals.
  • Controls the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • Approves emergency expenditure.

Executive Control

  • Question Hour — members question ministers.
  • No-Confidence Motion — can remove the government.
  • Censure Motion — expresses disapproval of a policy.
  • Adjournment Motion — draws attention to urgent matters.
  • Parliamentary Committees — examine the work of the executive.

Judicial Powers

  • Can impeach the President (Article 61).
  • Can remove judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Can punish members for breach of privilege.
  • Can decide on the disqualification of members.

Electoral Powers

  • Participates in the election of the President and Vice-President.
  • Can remove the Vice-President.

Constituent Powers

  • Can amend the Constitution (Article 368).
  • Can create new States and alter boundaries.
  • Can admit new States into the Union.

Other Powers

  • Approves emergency proclamations.
  • Approves international treaties.
  • Can create or abolish All India Services.
  • Can establish new courts and other bodies.

Anti-Defection Law — 10th Schedule

  • Added by the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985.
  • Defines defection — when an elected member voluntarily gives up membership of his party or votes/abstains against the party's direction.
  • A member who defects can be disqualified from the House.
  • The Speaker or Chairman of the House decides on disqualification.
  • Exception: A split by one-third of the members of a party was earlier allowed — but this was removed by the 91st Amendment (2003).
  • Now, only a merger of two-thirds of the members of a party is allowed.

Bihar Connection (BPSC Focus)

  • Bihar's representation in Lok Sabha: Bihar has 40 Lok Sabha seats — including reserved seats for SCs and STs.
  • Bihar's representation in Rajya Sabha: Bihar has 16 Rajya Sabha seats — elected by the Bihar Legislative Assembly.
  • Bihar Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha): 243 members; Bihar Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad): 75 members.
  • First Lok Sabha Speaker from Bihar: Bihar has produced several prominent Parliamentarians. Meira Kumar — the first woman Speaker of the Lok Sabha (2009–2014) — was born in Patna, Bihar. She is the daughter of Babu Jagjivan Ram, a former Deputy Prime Minister of India.
  • Babu Jagjivan Ram — a Dalit leader from Bihar — served as a Union Minister in various governments and was Deputy Prime Minister of India (1977–1979). He was a Member of Parliament for over five decades.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad (from Zeradei, Siwan, Bihar) was the President of the Constituent Assembly and later the first President of India.
  • Lal Bahadur Shastri — though born in Mughalsarai (UP), represented Allahabad in Lok Sabha — but was closely associated with Bihar's political culture.
  • Bihar has a bicameral legislature — Vidhan Sabha (243) and Vidhan Parishad (75). This is an important feature for BPSC.
  • Bihar's Lok Sabha seats since 2008: 40 seats — 34 general, 6 reserved for SCs, 0 for STs.
BPSC Must-Know: Bihar has 40 Lok Sabha seats and 16 Rajya Sabha seats. Meira Kumar — the first woman Speaker of the Lok Sabha — was born in Patna, Bihar. She is the daughter of Babu Jagjivan Ram, a Dalit leader from Bihar who served as Deputy Prime Minister of India. Bihar has a bicameral legislature — Vidhan Sabha (243) and Vidhan Parishad (75).
